# Product judgment

Loaded for shape, spec, and harden modes. These rules govern what to build and what to cut once the brief is filled.

- PJ-01 The smallest surface that completes the job wins. A larger surface is cost, not ambition. Propose the smallest interface that delivers the desired outcome and name what was deliberately left out.
- PJ-02 A feature that cannot state its consequence is cut. If the consequence field of the brief cannot be filled honestly, the feature does not ship in this design. Record the cut and the reason.
- PJ-03 Consistency with existing conventions beats isolated improvement. A local pattern that diverges from the product's established conventions needs evidence that the divergence pays for its novelty. Matching the product is the default.
- PJ-04 Every added step must buy clarity or safety. An extra step in a flow is justified only by a named gain in user clarity or protection from a named failure. Steps added "for structure" are removed.
- PJ-05 The empty state teaches. Design the first-run and cleared empty states to show what the surface does when populated. An empty state that says nothing is a missed explanation, not a neutral gap.
- PJ-06 Do not ask for a decision the product can make reversibly. If the product can pick a default and let the user change it later without loss, asking up front depletes attention for no safety gain.
- PJ-07 Confidence is stated, not implied. Every recommendation labels what is assumed, what is verified against evidence, and what is unknown. A confident-sounding design over unverified premises is a defect this rule exists to catch.
